Standard Diffie–Hellman Key Exchange

Core Cryptographic Principle & The Unauthenticated Dilemma

Diffie–Hellman allows two parties to establish a common secret without directly transmitting the secret key. Even an eavesdropper recording all network traffic cannot easily compute the secret without solving the computationally hard Discrete Logarithm Problem ($g^x \equiv Y \pmod p$).

Crucial Security Limitation: Basic Diffie–Hellman does NOT authenticate the communicating parties. Therefore, Diffie–Hellman alone is vulnerable to Man-in-the-Middle attacks.
