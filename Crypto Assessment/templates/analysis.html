Module 08 Theoretical & Practical Analysis

In-Depth Security Analysis

Rigorous examination of the mathematical foundation, advantages, structural limitations, and attack vectors of Diffie–Hellman.

Recommended Architecture
Cryptographic Advantages of Diffie–Hellman
Why DH revolutionized public-key cryptography in 1976
No Direct Transmission of Secret Key

Unlike symmetric key schemes requiring secure physical courier distribution, Diffie-Hellman generates the shared secret at each endpoint purely from public values. The secret key never travels over the wire.

Key Establishment Over Insecure Networks

Passive eavesdroppers observing the wire only record public values ($g, p, A, B$). Computing the secret $K$ requires solving the discrete logarithm of $A$ or $B$, which is computationally infeasible for sufficiently large primes.

Perfect Forward Secrecy (PFS) via Ephemeral Keys

When implemented with ephemeral keys (DHE / ECDHE), unique private values ($a, b$) are generated per session and immediately discarded from memory. If long-term server identity keys are compromised in the future, past recorded sessions cannot be decrypted retroactively.

Mathematical Symmetry & Flexibility

Both parties perform identical mathematical exponentiations: $(g^b)^a \equiv (g^a)^b \pmod p$. Can be computed over modular integer groups (MODP) or elliptic curve groups (ECDH) for extreme efficiency.

Critical Limitations & Vulnerabilities
Inherent flaws in unauthenticated Diffie–Hellman
Complete Lack of Authentication

Basic Diffie–Hellman exchanges mathematical values without any identity verification. Alice has no mathematical guarantee that public value $B$ came from Bob rather than an attacker.

Fatal Vulnerability to Active MITM Exploits

An attacker (Eve) on the routing path can intercept $A$ and $B$, replace them with $A_E$ and $B_E$, and split the conversation into two separate encrypted channels, giving Eve full plaintext visibility and tampering power.

Parameter Weaknesses & Small Subgroup Attacks

If non-safe primes or unverified generators are chosen, malicious actors can force the public value into a small subgroup of order $q$, leaking bits of the victim's private key (e.g., Logjam attack / CVE-2015-4000).

Vulnerability to Quantum Computing (Shor's Algorithm)

Both finite-field DH and Elliptic Curve DH rely on the hardness of the discrete logarithm. A fault-tolerant quantum computer running Shor's algorithm can solve discrete logs in polynomial time $O((\log N)^3)$, rendering classical DH completely obsolete.

Mathematical Mechanics of the Man-in-the-Middle Exploit

Consider the algebraic steps during an active MITM interception over prime field $\mathbb{F}_p$:

1. Alice ↔ Eve Channel
Alice sends: $A = g^a \pmod p$
Eve intercepts $A$, sends: $B_E = g^{e_2} \pmod p$
Alice computes:
$K_{Alice} = (B_E)^a = (g^{e_2})^a = g^{a \cdot e_2} \pmod p$
Eve computes:
$K_{EA} = A^{e_2} = (g^a)^{e_2} = g^{a \cdot e_2} \pmod p$
∴ $K_{Alice} \equiv K_{EA}$
2. Eve ↔ Bob Channel
Eve sends: $A_E = g^{e_1} \pmod p$
Bob receives $A_E$, sends: $B = g^b \pmod p$
Eve intercepts $B$
Bob computes:
$K_{Bob} = (A_E)^b = (g^{e_1})^b = g^{b \cdot e_1} \pmod p$
Eve computes:
$K_{EB} = B^{e_1} = (g^b)^{e_1} = g^{b \cdot e_1} \pmod p$
∴ $K_{Bob} \equiv K_{EB}$
3. The Fatal Discrepancy
Because $e_1 \neq b$ and $e_2 \neq a$ in general:
$g^{a \cdot e_2} \not\equiv g^{b \cdot e_1} \pmod p$
∴ $K_{Alice} \neq K_{Bob}$

Alice and Bob cannot communicate directly. Every packet must be decrypted by Eve using $K_{EA}$, reviewed/altered, and re-encrypted with $K_{EB}$.
