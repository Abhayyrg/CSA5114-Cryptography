A cryptographic key fingerprint is a compact hash of a public key or parameter. Communicating parties compare fingerprints (via an out-of-band channel like a phone call, secure QR code, or published directory) to verify authenticity.

Binds communicating entities to verified asymmetric keypairs. The sender proves possession of a private key without revealing it.

Alice signs her ephemeral DH public value ($A$) with her private signing key. Bob verifies it with Alice's public key; if Eve modifies $A$, signature verification fails.

Electronic credentials binding a domain identity (e.g., bank.com) to a public key, signed by a trusted Certification Authority (CA).

The hierarchical ecosystem of Root CAs, Intermediate CAs, registration authorities, and revocation lists that enables worldwide trust without direct pre-shared secrets.

Clients verify that the server's certificate is unexpired, issued by a trusted Root in the client's store, not revoked via OCSP/CRL, and matches the requested hostname.

Cryptographic hashes displayed in human-readable hex/visual art (used extensively in SSH host verification, Signal safety numbers, and PGP keyservers).
